Turn a spare laptop into a home security camera.

Leave a laptop at home, arm it, and it watches the room. When something moves it saves a snapshot, records a clip if the movement continues, and sends both to your phone on Telegram.

Getting started

Three things, then it's watching.

The whole of setup is one screen with three numbered steps. Nothing here needs a terminal, and nothing here needs a technical background.

1

Get alerts on your phone

Create your own Telegram bot, paste in the token and chat ID, and press Test to confirm they work. About five minutes, once, with a guide that walks you through it.

2

Choose your camera

The picker lists only the devices that actually hand over a frame, so a virtual camera from OBS or Iriun cannot quietly take the slot your real webcam should be in.

3

Start watching

One button. The status screen tells you in plain English what is happening, and you can stop it again the same way.

When something moves

A snapshot straight away, then the clip.

Motion sends a snapshot immediately, so you know something happened before you know what. If the movement carries on past the minimum duration you set, a clip records and follows it. That clip includes a few seconds from before the movement started, so you see what walked in rather than joining halfway through.

A cooldown keeps a busy afternoon from becoming a hundred notifications. Everything is saved on the laptop as well, and deleted automatically after 30 days by default.

Settings you can actually answer

No thresholds. No contour areas. No guessing.

Motion detection is decided by two numbers that mean nothing to somebody pointing a camera at their porch. So the app asks the question you can answer, and writes the numbers itself.

Only big movements

Ignores pets, shadows and swaying branches. You will get the fewest alerts.

Recommended Default

Picks up a person walking into view. A good starting point for most homes.

Small movements too

Catches pets and small changes. Expect more alerts, including false ones.

The same treatment applies to the live view, where frames per second and JPEG quality become Easy on the laptop, Recommended and Smoothest picture. The raw numbers are still there under an “Advanced numbers” disclosure if you want them. They are just not the way in.

Day and night

A room at noon and the same room at 19:00 are not the same room.

So they don't share one sensitivity setting. Pick a level for daytime and a different one for night, set the hours the night profile applies, and the app switches between them on schedule.

Text it back

Message your bot /record to start a clip, /stop to end it, or /get to list what is saved and pull one up. Only the chat you configured is obeyed; anything from anywhere else is discarded.

Set it and forget it

Open when you log in, and start watching as soon as it opens. Two separate switches, because wanting one does not mean wanting both. Registered for your user only, so no administrator prompt.

Browse what it caught

Videos and photos, newest first, with thumbnails and sizes, each marked as something you started or something that moved. Delete it, open it, or send it to Telegram again.

Any camera it can see

The built-in webcam, a USB camera you plugged in, or a better camera already on your network by its RTSP or HTTP address. Network cameras get their own box, since they never show up in a scan of local devices.

Privacy: read this first

This records video of a room. Here is exactly what that means.

This section is near the top of the README too. A camera in your home is not something to be casual about, and a page that buried this would be selling you a different product.

01

Footage leaves your machine

Delivering clips and snapshots to you means uploading them to Telegram's servers. There is no way around that while Telegram is the delivery route.

02

Other people have not agreed to this

Point it at a shared flat, an office, or a room with guests in it and you are recording them. Depending on where you live, doing that without telling them may be illegal, whoever owns the camera or the home.

03

Your bot token is a password

Anyone who gets it can receive your footage and command your camera. It is stored with owner-only permissions, and there is no shared bot and no token baked into the binary. Every install talks to a bot you made.

04

Clips stay on the laptop as well

For 30 days by default, deleted automatically after that, or kept forever if you ask. Anyone with access to your user account can watch them.

Download

Free, open source, and nothing to sign up for.

Platform Architecture Install
Windows 10 / 11 x64 (Intel/AMD) Run the installer and follow the wizard. It installs for your user only, so it never asks for administrator rights.
Debian / Ubuntu x64 (amd64) sudo apt install ./kaa-rada_*_amd64.deb. Use apt rather than dpkg -i: the package declares OpenCV and WebKit, so apt pulls them in for you, while dpkg only reports them missing.
Other Linux x64 (amd64) Extract the tarball, then ./install.sh. Needs OpenCV 4.6, GTK 3.24+ and WebKit2GTK 4.1+ from your distro.
Get the latest release Source on GitHub

You will also need a webcam that no other program is currently using, and your own Telegram bot. The setup guide covers creating one.

The binaries are unsigned. On Windows, SmartScreen will warn you that the publisher is unknown and you will have to choose to run it anyway. Code-signing certificates are a recurring cost this project has not taken on. Every download ships with a .sha256 file if you want to verify it yourself.

macOS is not supported, deliberately rather than by oversight: shipping a macOS build that will open at all requires paid Apple Developer enrollment, so it is deferred instead of half-done. Telegram is currently the only alerting channel.
